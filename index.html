The Open-Source Guide to Surviving a STEM Degree

Science, Technology, Engineering, and Math (STEM) degrees are notoriously rigorous. The attrition rate for first-year engineering and computer science students is historically high because the transition from high school memorization to university-level problem solving is brutal. To survive and thrive in a STEM major, you must upgrade your operating system.

1. Treat Math as a Language, Not a Chore

Many students struggle in STEM because they view Calculus and Linear Algebra as hurdles to jump over rather than the fundamental language of their discipline. You cannot understand machine learning algorithms, thermodynamics, or quantum mechanics if you are mathematically illiterate. Spend disproportionate time mastering your foundational math courses during your freshman and sophomore years. It pays massive dividends later.

2. Practice "Version Control" with Your Assignments

Whether you are writing code for a Computer Science lab or drafting a 20-page lab report for Chemistry, never rely on a single, continuously saved file. Embrace version control (like Git) or simply duplicate your files iteratively (e.g., `LabReport_v1`, `LabReport_v2`). This prevents catastrophic data loss if a file corrupts or if you realize your most recent structural changes completely broke your project.

3. The 15-Minute Struggle Rule

Avoid the "Instant Answer" Trap

When you hit a roadblock in a complex problem set, do not immediately look up the solution on Chegg or ask a TA. Force yourself to struggle with the problem for exactly 15 minutes. Review your textbook, trace your logic, and attempt alternative methods. The neuroplasticity required for deep learning happens during the struggle, not when you read the final answer.

4. Build Portfolios Over GPAs

While maintaining a strong GPA is necessary for graduate school, tech and engineering employers care far more about what you can build. An applicant with a 3.2 GPA and a portfolio of personal coding projects, CAD designs, or published research will almost always beat an applicant with a 4.0 GPA and zero practical experience. Start building things outside the classroom immediately.
